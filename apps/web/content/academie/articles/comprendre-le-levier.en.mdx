Leverage lets you control a position larger than the capital you actually put up. With 1:30 leverage, 1,000 EUR of margin drives 30,000 EUR of exposure. Appealing on paper, formidable in practice.

## The mechanism

The broker lends the difference between your margin and the position size. As long as the market moves your way, the gain is computed on the full position, not on your margin alone. The reverse effect is just as mechanical.

<Callout variant="attention">
A market move that looks tiny becomes heavy once multiplied by leverage. An adverse 3% move on a 1:30 leveraged position wipes out almost an entire unprotected margin.
</Callout>

## Why position size comes first

The real safeguard is not the leverage on offer but the position size you choose. Risking a fixed, modest percentage of your capital per trade neutralises most of the danger, whatever leverage the broker provides.

Leverage is neither good nor bad in itself: it is an amplifier. It makes sound risk management even more effective, and poor management even more costly.
