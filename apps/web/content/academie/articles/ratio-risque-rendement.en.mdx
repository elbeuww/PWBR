Before opening a position, one question matters more than any other: what am I risking, and what am I aiming for in return? The risk/reward ratio (often written R:R) places those two amounts side by side.

<Figure
  src="/images/academie/ratio-risque-rendement-fig.png"
  alt="Entry, stop-loss and take-profit on a chart"
  caption="Risk (entry→stop) versus reward (entry→target)."

## Defining risk and reward

Risk is the distance between your entry and your stop-loss. Reward is the distance between your entry and your target (take-profit). A 1:3 R:R means you accept losing one unit to try to gain three.

<Callout variant="info">
The R:R alone says nothing about how likely the trade is to work. A pretty 1:5 on an unlikely scenario is still a poor trade.
</Callout>

## A worked example

<TradeExample
  instrument="EUR/USD"
  sens="buy"

  objectif="1.0940"
/>

Here the risk is 30 pips and the target 90 pips, a 1:3 R:R. Even with only four winning trades out of ten, this profile stays viable over time because the gains far outweigh the losses.

## What the ratio does not replace

A good ratio replaces neither discipline nor a stop-loss that is actually honoured. Moving your stop to dodge a loss turns a stated 1:3 into an open-ended bet. Consistency between the plan you announce and the plan you execute matters as much as the number itself.
